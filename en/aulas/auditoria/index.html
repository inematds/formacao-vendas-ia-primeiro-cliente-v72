Lesson 3 · 22 min

The AI audit in 4 steps

The AI audit is the most effective service for getting into a company that has never heard of you. The client walks away with a document they keep, show to their partner, and use to decide: what AI the company already uses, what risks that brings, and what to do in the next 30 days. And it starts before the first question, with written authorization.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Agree on scope and written authorization before looking at anything
  2. 2Research the company and show up with hypotheses ready
  3. 3Build the inventory of apps and agents, including the AI that IT doesn't know about
  4. 4Deliver the 7-part report with a 30-day plan

Goal: conduct an AI audit with written authorization, an inventory of apps and agents, risks, and a report with a 30-day plan.

01 · The audit path

One agreement and four steps, always in this order. Click each one.

It is the front door of this program and the bridge to the Auditor program of Professional 2030.

The audit doesn't end with itself: it generates the sales that follow, the fix, and the training.

02 · The four steps, up close

What to do and the common mistake in each one.

1. Research

Run the research prompt with the company name in a variable: change one line and reuse the text dozens of times.

Look for the assumption markers. If there are none, the model is presenting a guess as fact.

2. Inventory

IT lists what it bought. Employees use extensions, chatbots on personal accounts, and recording apps that nobody approved: that's shadow AI.

A short internal survey ("how do you use technology at work?") reveals what the official list hides.

FormUse a form only when the client came to you. If you were the one who reached out, asking for homework before proving value kills the conversation.

3. Risks and solutions

Data: customer records in an open spreadsheet or going to a vendor with no contract. Permissions: an agent that sends, deletes, or gives discounts without approval. Keys: a password or access key visible in the code or shared around.

Each drafted fix says what would stay manual. "Nothing, everything automated" is a fantasy.

4. Report

With the 7-part structure, you can answer the "and what do I get at the end?" question by email.

Write by dictating: say what you concluded, transcribe it, and ask the assistant to structure it. Your real opinion stays in the text.

04 · When to decline, and free or paid

The audit is also your filter. And honesty is what closes the deal.

1. Rotten data

The same information written five different ways, duplicate records, a field nobody can explain.

Sell the data-organizing service first: less glamorous, more of a sure thing.

2. System with no integration

Say how much the workaround costs. If the client won't accept it, decline: blocked access eats up weeks through no fault of yours.

3. Resistant team

The team avoids detailing how they work, afraid of being replaced, or nobody below the owner was told.

Propose training before automation: people who have learned to use it stop sabotaging it.

4. Free or paid?

Can't yet generate meetings on your own? The free audit is your way in and your practice. Already have a repeatable process and the client came to you? Charge.

At the end, educate without locking in: say where this kind of project usually goes wrong, even if they carry it out with someone else.

PhraseIf you carry out this plan with another company, that's fine. Pay attention to these five points: that's where this kind of project usually goes wrong.

05 · Practice at Horizonte Distribuidora

Horizonte is a fictional company of 40 people, with study AI pieces. Everything there is fake, and on purpose.

1 · The authorization form

15 min

Fill out the scope and authorization form for Horizonte, with the owner (Carlos Mendes) as the person who authorizes. Then read aloud the sentence about what the audit is not.

Done when: the form has who authorizes, what you will look at, what you will not do, period and deliverable, and the sentence "it is not a pentest, nor incident response, nor legal advice" is written down and was said out loud.

2 · Inventory and one-page report

45 min

Open the IT inventory and the lab pieces. Build the inventory with the template, including the tools that show up only in the internal survey. Name one data risk, one permissions risk and one keys risk, each with where you saw it. Close with the 30-day plan.

Done when: the inventory has every piece with an owner and the official/shadow mark; there are 3 risks (data, permissions, keys) with evidence; and the 30-day plan has one task per week. Finding all the planted flaws is left for the Auditor program.
