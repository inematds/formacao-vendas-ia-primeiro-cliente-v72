Lesson 5 · 17 min

A list that converts

Without a routine, prospecting turns into a burst: thirty calls on a Monday, frustration, and two weeks of silence. This lesson builds what holds up everything else: where to find companies, how to qualify each one in three minutes, how to find the name of the decision-maker, and a color-coded spreadsheet that shows your whole day on one screen.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Why calls, not email, for someone starting from zero
  2. 2Find and qualify companies in three minutes, without talking to anyone
  3. 3Find the decision-maker's name in public sources
  4. 4The color spreadsheet and a routine that doesn't depend on mood

Goal: build a qualified list of companies, with the name of the decision-maker, and a daily contact routine.

01 · From search to the day's list

Five steps, once a day. Tap each one.

Click each block in the diagram to see what it does.

Simplicity and consistency beat expensive tools: don't subscribe to a sales system before your first client.

02 · The three filters, up close

Qualifying means answering one question: does this company lose something because of the problem I solve?

1. Right size

Rule of thumb: between 10 and 50 reviews. Under 10, they are too small or too new. Over 300, they already have an agency and you are not talking to the decision-maker.

And ask whether your service makes sense in that region before you build the list.

2. The gap

For the AI audit, the hook is seeing that the company already uses AI or automation: a bot on the website, automatic customer service on WhatsApp, an order app, a customer area.

If they have something running and nobody has checked it, you have found the subject of your call.

In the spreadsheet"Gap" column: "WhatsApp bot with no human in sight", "order app with customer login", "only a long form".

3. The math that justifies the call

A decent service website converts about 2% of visits into contacts. A thousand visits and two contacts is 0.2%: ten times below that.

You are not accusing anyone: you show the math and ask whether some people have not found a comfortable way to reach them.

4. The rules of your market

Phone prospecting is regulated in many places: calling hours, opt-out records, do-not-call lists, and significant fines.

Check what applies in your country and in your industry. Respecting people who asked not to be contacted is a rule, not a courtesy.

03 · Why calls, and the routine

If you are starting from zero, you do not have a scale problem: you have a learning problem.

1. A call gives you an answer in seconds

You hear the real objection, with the tone of voice along with it, and every call trains you for the meeting that comes next.

Email takes weeks to prepare, solves a scale problem you do not have, and silence teaches you nothing.

2. The daily goal

The full goal is 75 companies a day. If you have another job, start with 20, but every business day, no exceptions.

Dial twice: some people pick up on the second try.

3. Color before the next number

One color and one line of notes. Five calls later you do not remember which was which, and you call again someone who asked you not to call.

4. The math that calms you down

The exact rate does not matter: after two weeks you know how many calls produce a meeting.

Then a "no" stops being rejection and becomes a line in the spreadsheet, on the way to the next yes.

TipCall alongside another person who is also just starting, on a video call: you correct each other's script and quitting drops sharply.

04 · Templates to copy

The spreadsheet columns and the prompt that adapts the qualification to your niche.

Spreadsheet columns
company | main phone | decision-maker's name | website | reviews | estimated traffic | gap (your hook) | status (color) | note (one line) | next contact

COLORS
yellow = no answer, call back tomorrow
red    = not interested, remove from list (and never call again)
blue   = asked for a call back later: <day and time>
green  = meeting booked
Prompt · niche qualification manual
You are my pre-sales operator. I am going to prospect companies
in a sector by phone to offer an AI audit (inventory of what the company
already uses in AI, data risks, permissions and keys, 30-day plan).
MY NICHE = <sector>
MY REGION = <city or state>
MY ENTRY PRICE = <amount>

Deliver:
1. 8 objective checks, using public information only, that indicate whether the
   company already uses AI or automation with nobody checking it. Yes/no and weight.
2. 5 signs that I should NOT spend a call on that company.
3. 4 public sources to find the decision-maker in this sector and region.
4. The spreadsheet columns with an example of a filled-in row.
5. Sector particularities: seasonality, the owner's schedule,
   vocabulary, and contact rules or restrictions that usually exist.
6. In two sentences, how I explain the reason for the call without accusing anyone.
If you do not have a basis to state something, say it is an assumption.

05 · Practice

A good list gets roughly one in three companies approved.

1 · List of 20 companies

60 min

Run the qualification prompt and test the checklist on three real companies in your niche. If they all pass or all fail, adjust the criteria. Then build the spreadsheet with 20 companies.

Done when: the spreadsheet has 20 qualified companies, each with the gap noted and the name of the decision-maker (or the source where you will look for it).

2 · Rules and routine

20 min

Write down the phone contact rules that apply in your country and your industry. Choose a fixed time for your daily block and set your starting goal.

Done when: the contact rules are noted with their source, and the daily block is on your calendar, with a time and a goal (for example, 20 calls).
