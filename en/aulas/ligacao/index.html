Lesson 6 · 20 min

Cold call with permission

The most expensive mistake in phone prospecting is trying to sell the service on the call. You are interrupting someone in the middle of their workday, and nobody buys anything that way. The call has only one goal: to book the meeting. This lesson teaches you to ask for permission, talk about results, hold back the details, and leave with a time booked.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1The first 15 seconds: honesty and permission
  2. 2The one-sentence pitch: result + deny the wrong assumption
  3. 3Hold back the details and turn to the meeting
  4. 4Practice on a simulated call with a scorecard

Goal: call by asking for permission, talk about results, and book the meeting without explaining the service over the phone.

01 · The anatomy of the call

Five moves, always in this order. Tap each one.

You don't sell the service, you sell the meeting. The service gets sold there, with a shared screen and their full attention.

    03 · Templates to copy

    The scripts stay open on your desk. You run the two prompts before burning real numbers.

    Opening, pitch, and pivot
    OPENING (up to 15 s)
— Hi, this is <your name>. Am I speaking with <owner's name>?
— <Name>, I'll be honest: this is a cold call, I want to sell you
  something. Will you give me fifteen seconds to tell you what it is, or would you
  rather hang up now?

PITCH (1 sentence: result + deny the wrong assumption)
— We help <type of company> <result in owner's language>,
  without <the wrong assumption: switching systems / spending on ads>.

PIVOT ("how does it work?")
— Look, I called you out of the blue, so I'll be direct: the best way to
  understand it is to see it. My goal was just to find out if it's worth setting up fifteen
  minutes for me to show you. Does that make sense to you?

CLOSE
— Today at <4 p.m.> or tomorrow at <10 a.m.>?
— What's the best email? I'll send you the invitation right now.
    Front desk: the three situations
    ["is he expecting your call?" / "what is it about?"]
— I'll be honest: it's a cold call. We help <type of company>
  <result>, and I wanted to know if that's a priority for you all right now.

["he's not available"]
— No problem. Is there a better time for me to call back? First thing in the morning,
  when he arrives, would that work?

["want to leave a message?"]
— Yes. Just say it's about <result>, and that I'll call back
  <day and time>.

Write down the name of whoever answered. Next time: "hi, <name>, it's <your name>
again, has <owner> arrived yet?"
    Prompt · script for your niche
    You are my phone prospecting coach. Write my cold call script
to book a 15-minute meeting.
MY NICHE = <industry>
WHAT I SELL = <e.g.: AI audit: what the company already uses, risks and a 30-day plan>
THE RESULT FOR THE CLIENT = <in owner's language>
THE WRONG ASSUMPTION this industry makes = <e.g.: "he's going to sell me a new system">

Structure, with the lines ready:
1. OPENING (max. 15 s): who I am + cold call + permission + explicit way out.
2. PITCH (1 sentence): result + denial of the wrong assumption.
3. ANSWER TO "how does it work?": without explaining, pivoting to the meeting.
   End with "does that make sense?".
4. CLOSE: two time options, same day or next day.
5. FRONT DESK: "what is it about?", "he's not in", "want to leave a message?".
6. OBJECTIONS: the 6 most common in this industry, each answer in at most
   two sentences, going back to booking.
7. WHAT TO NEVER SAY: forbidden words and topics.
Spoken language, short sentences, zero jargon. Fits on one page.
    Prompt · call simulator
    Let's practice a cold call. You are the owner of <type of company in my
niche>, busy, in the middle of the workday, and you just picked up the phone
without expecting this call.
Rules:
- ALWAYS answer in one or two sentences, like a person in a hurry.
- 6 out of 10 times, resist or try to end the call.
- If I use jargon, react with confusion.
- If I explain too much, cut me off with "look, I don't have time right now".
- Do not break character until I write FIM.
When I write FIM, rate me on five points:
(1) did I ask permission in the first 15 s? (2) did I talk about results or
technology? (3) did I hold back information? (4) did I offer two times?
(5) what was my weakest sentence, and how do I rewrite it?
Start by saying only "Hello?".

    04 · Words and breathing

    Objections and closing in depth are in courses 3 and 5 of the program. Here, the minimum for the call.

    1. Words that work

    "less risk", "without switching your system", "fifteen minutes", "I'll show you", "does that make sense?".

    2. Words that kill

    "synergy", "digital transformation", "revolutionary", "disruptive" and technical names that the person answering doesn't use.

    3. Accept the first bad ones

    To get good, you have to be bad first. Replace the "ummm" with silence: a pause sounds like confidence.

    If it's allowed where you are, record your calls and listen to one a day.

    05 · Practice

    Twenty minutes of practice saves twenty burned calls.

    1 · One-page script

    30 min

    Run the script prompt for your niche. Read it all out loud and rewrite every sentence you wouldn't say to a friend. Then do five rounds in the simulator.

    Done when: the script fits on one page, the opening lasts up to 15 timed seconds, and no answer to an objection runs longer than two sentences.

    2 · Simulated call with a score

    10 min

    Do the simulated call for this lesson. If you don't reach the top score, read the comments, click restart, and try again.

    Done when: you finished the simulated call with the top score and you can say why asking for the person by name and offering two time slots make a difference.

    07 · Summary

    One single goal: the meetingThe service is sold there, not over the phone.
    Permission in 15 secondsAdmitting it's a cold call disarms the person who answers.
    Result + denied assumptionIt's the combination that creates curiosity.
    Two time slots, as soon as possibleAnd the invitation goes out before the next number.
